Dear Editors,

We are submitting our manuscript entitled "PhyloPower: a Python package for phylogeny-aware study design in microbiome community analysis" for consideration as a Methods paper in Nucleic Acids Research.

Microbiome studies increasingly rely on phylogeny-aware beta-diversity metrics, yet the tools used to plan those studies remain designed for non-phylogenetic taxonomic profiles. Simulation-based power analysis can prospectively evaluate study designs beyond the single effect observed in a pilot dataset, but no existing framework supports the newer structure-aware metrics—Gemelli's Phylogenetic-RPCA and PhyloFunc—or taxon-function coupled data structures such as those produced by metaproteomics. PhyloPower closes this gap.

The contribution of this work is threefold. First, PhyloPower is, to our knowledge, the first power-analysis framework that natively accommodates both taxonomic abundance profiles and taxon-function coupled multi-omics data, integrating the phylogenetic-tree-based distances these communities actually use. Second, its data-type-specific generators (PCAM and MDC-TF-MC) construct empirically informed synthetic sample pools that preserve the structures these distances exploit—phylogenetically structured covariance and bipartite taxon-function support—while bidirectional effect modulation generates continuous, realized-omega-squared-indexed effect gradients in both directions around the pilot effect. Third, the framework returns the minimum per-group sample size for any user-specified target effect size and power, and uniquely propagates reference-tree estimation errors through the full workflow as a built-in sensitivity analysis. Type I error calibration, pool-fidelity audits, and validation on three independent public cohorts (QinJ_2012, YachidaS_2019, and PRIDE PXD069517) demonstrate that the framework is calibrated and generalizes beyond the datasets used for its development.

We believe this work fits NAR's Methods scope: it addresses a widely recognized resource-allocation problem in microbiome study design, provides a novel methodological capability with clear utility to the broad genomics and computational-biology readership of the journal, and is released as open-source software with documentation, a tutorial, and archived validation materials.

PhyloPower is freely available at https://github.com/Yutongli534/PhyloPower and archived at Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21991013).

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration by any other journal. All authors have read and approved the submission and declare no competing interests.
